About JuDDGES

Judicial Decision Data Gathering, Encoding and Sharing — an Open Science platform for scalable, transparent, human-validated judicial decision analysis.
NLP + HITL Open Data & Software Cross-jurisdiction

Project Overview

The overall goal of the JuDDGES (Judicial Decision Data Gathering, Encoding and Sharing) project is to harness state-of-the-art Natural Language Processing and Human-In-The-Loop (HITL) technologies to provide legal researchers with open software and tools that enable extensive, flexible, and on-going meta-annotation capability — combining automated methods with domain experts in-the-loop.

This capability is applied to legal records and judgments from criminal courts across jurisdictions with varied legal constitutions, including England & Wales, Poland, and partner work in Lyon (France).

Use-case: criminal judgments Method: expert-validated annotation Outcome: reusable datasets

Why This Matters

We seek to dissolve barriers of resources, language, and data/format inhomogeneity that currently impede research on judicial decision-making. By making the software, tools, and resulting data resources openly available, researchers are empowered to develop and empirically test theories of judicial decision-making and address practice-relevant judicial policy questions.

The project supports Open Science in a domain that has historically been slow to adopt open research data and open software principles. The resulting annotated pan-national dataset and toolset will constitute a major open and reusable legal research repository in Europe for judicial decision-making research.

How the Platform Works (AI + Human-in-the-Loop)

Workflow

  1. Ingest legal decisions (unstructured text) into a searchable corpus
  2. Suggest candidate topics/codes using NLP models
  3. Validate with expert review using a HITL loop (accept / reject / correct)
  4. Audit decisions and maintain traceability for research quality
  5. Export structured datasets for analysis (CSV/SPSS-ready)
AI accelerates discovery, while human expertise protects validity and interpretability.

What you can do

Search at scale Meta-annotate Compare jurisdictions Track decisions Reuse & share
Tip: Designed for non-AI specialist legal researchers — simple interface, research-grade traceability.

Powered by Margin-Aware Active Learning

This platform is built around a Human-in-the-Loop learning loop where the system prioritises the most informative decisions for expert review. Practically, this means the tool can focus reviewer attention on ambiguous/low-confidence cases to improve consistency and reduce annotation cost over time.

Core concept: margin-aware active learning for user-adaptive text classification — view paper (PDF)
Active learning Uncertainty / margin sampling User-adaptive HITL governance

For Research Teams & Institutions

Beyond a single study, the platform is designed as reusable research infrastructure. It can support research groups, public legal institutions, and collaborative teams that need scalable document search, annotation governance, and structured data extraction — without requiring a bespoke AI pipeline per project.

Who it’s for

  • Empirical legal researchers and ECRs
  • Cross-institution research consortia
  • Courts / justice bodies exploring evidence-led policy
  • Data teams needing structured exports and audit trails

What it delivers

  • Configurable coding schemes and topic workflows
  • Human validation loop for model suggestions
  • Project-based organisation and collaborative workflow
  • Exports for statistical analysis and reporting
Optional pathway: hosted deployments for research labs + institutional teams, with managed onboarding, private corpora, and governance controls.
